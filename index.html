02 The platform

From flight planning to inspection reports.

DroneOS ties the whole job together: plan a mission, fly it, bring the data home, work out what it shows, and produce a report someone can act on. PX4 still flies the drone.

Everything runs on site. Planning, data processing and reporting happen on a single box next to the array, so a mission does not depend on an internet connection being available.

  • One system, not five tools Flight, data transfer, analysis, approval and reporting live in the same workflow, so nothing is lost between vendors or exports.
  • Works without a connection Captured data is processed on site. Unreliable links stop being a scheduling problem.
  • A person decides The system proposes an inspection route; an operator reviews and approves it before anything is flown.
  • Reports you can trace back Each finding links to the exact image it came from and the mission it came from.

02.1 What you get

Four things DroneOS does for you

01

Plan and fly the mission

Build a route over the asset, watch it in real time, and follow progress while it flies.

02

Bring the data home safely

After landing, captured data is transferred to the Field Box and checked before anything is analysed.

03

Analyse and propose

The system turns the captured data into a proposed inspection route, ready for a person to review.

04

Report with evidence

A finished report lists each finding, the image behind it, and the mission it came from.

Technical detail How this is implemented under the hood

Mission orchestration — waypoint-based missions, dynamic route generation, live path preview, and staging boundaries. Mission identity and revision are carried end to end so a stale plan cannot be flown.

Safety and integrity — position-dependent commands fail closed when telemetry is stale, route and geofence geometry are validated before upload, mission execution identity is preserved, and emergency LAND/RTL behaviour is retained.

Postflight pipeline — deterministic handoff with pinned descriptors, identity validation and SHA-256 checksums at every transfer boundary, plus no-overwrite publication into the durable artifact ledger.

Operator visibility — live telemetry and state, route review, and explicit confirmation of the proposal fingerprint and mission revision before Flight B is staged.

03 End-to-end workflow

Seven steps, two flights, one decision

The same sequence runs for every job. The drone flies twice — once to map the asset, once to inspect it — and a person approves the route in between. After each flight, the captured data is transferred and checked before anything is analysed or reported.

01

Recon flight

A mapping pass over the whole array to see what is there.

02

Landing & data transfer

After landing, the captured data is copied to the Field Box and checked.

03

Local analysis

The data is processed on site into a panel map.

04

Operator approval

An operator reviews and approves the proposed inspection route.

05

Inspection flight

The approved route is flown to capture close-up imagery.

06

Landing & data transfer

The inspection data is transferred to the Field Box and checked.

07

Report

Findings are listed with the image and mission behind each one.

Why it matters

Nothing moves to the next step automatically. A dataset that fails its check stops there, and the inspection route is never staged until a person has approved it.

04 Architecture

DroneOS / Jetson Field Box / Onboard / PX4

Clear separation of concerns. PX4 remains the flight authority. DroneOS coordinates, validates and reports; it does not override PX4 flight control.

Layer 01

Operator

The person in charge

Plans the mission, watches it fly, and approves the inspection route before it goes. Nothing is launched automatically.

Interface
Layer 02

DroneOS

Mission and data layer

The software that ties the job together. It runs the inspection workflow, checks the data coming back, and builds the report.

Orchestration
Layer 03

Jetson Field Box

The box on site

A small computer (NVIDIA Jetson Orin Nano) that runs DroneOS next to the solar array. Planning, data processing and storage all happen here.

Edge Node
Layer 04

Onboard system

On the drone

The camera and its supporting software on the aircraft itself. It captures the imagery, then hands the data over to the Field Box.

Payload/Vehicle
Layer 05

PX4 Autopilot

The flight controller

PX4 keeps flight authority. It flies the aircraft, holds the safety envelope and handles its own failsafes. DroneOS asks it to do things — it never takes over the controls.

Flight Authority
  • PX4 keeps flight authority at all times.
  • An operator approves every mission.
  • Data is processed on site.
  • Nothing proceeds on stale or unverified data.

05 Initial use case

Solar panel inspections

A solar array is a good first target: the panels repeat, they need regular checking, and a defect has to be documented rather than just spotted. The same seven-step workflow applies, with the drone flying the array twice.

Flight 1 of 2

Mapping the array

  1. The drone flies a wide pass over the whole array
  2. It lands and the captured data moves to the Field Box
  3. The data is checked, then turned into a panel map
  4. A close-up inspection route is proposed from that map
  5. An operator reviews and approves the route
Flight 2 of 2

Inspecting and reporting

  1. The approved route is flown at close range
  2. It lands and the inspection data moves to the Field Box
  3. That data is checked, then analysed for findings
  4. A report lists each finding with the image and mission behind it

What is not yet shown: the capture and analysis steps are validated in simulation, not on real imagery. See Status & Vision for exactly what has and has not been validated.

06 Status & Vision

What works today, and what comes next

The platform is in active development. Everything on the left has been exercised in simulation; everything on the right is planned work that has not been validated yet.

Working today

Validated in simulation
  • The full seven-step inspection workflow, from mapping flight to report.
  • Mission preparation, upload to the autopilot, automated flight and landing.
  • Data transfer after each flight, with a check that stops the job if something is wrong.
  • Operator review and approval before the inspection route is ever staged.
  • Reports where every finding can be traced back to the image and mission it came from.
  • Automatic test suites covering the safety and failure paths.
  • A local-first setup that runs on a Field Box computer as well as a laptop.

Still to come

Not yet validated
  • Real flights under defined test conditions.
  • Real cameras and capture hardware on the aircraft.
  • Real solar-field imagery, and the analysis that turns it into findings.
  • Thermal and visual defect detection with production-grade accuracy.
  • Hardening the Field Box for dependable long-term field use.
  • Extended recovery behaviour if something goes wrong in the field.

How far along this really is

The work so far has been validated in a flight simulator (PX4 with Gazebo), not in the air. So the workflow, the data handoffs and the safety behaviour have been tested, while the aircraft, the cameras and the defect detection are still development targets. We are not claiming real-flight validation, certified functional safety, production cybersecurity, autonomous obstacle avoidance, commercial readiness or regulatory approval.

Validation environment and technical detail

Validation ran against PX4 v1.16.2 SITL with Gazebo, Python 3.12, and Docker for the backend and dashboard. A laptop-hosted simulator is development and test infrastructure, not the intended Field Box product.

  • Mission identity, revision and checksum integrity (SHA-256) are enforced; mismatches stop the job.
  • Report and workflow state names used by the runtime include REPORT_READY and its pre-staging states.
  • Fail-closed boundaries and bounded recovery are covered by the safe test suite.
  • Capture currently uses deterministic demo providers in place of physical drone-side camera hardware.
